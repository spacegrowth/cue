What lands in Cue

Three things an agent needs from you

Cue hears about each one the moment it happens, through the agents' own hooks, and you answer right there. The terminal keeps working too: answer in either place and the other catches up.

YOUR TURN

A finished turn

Read what it said and reply from Cue. Your message is typed into that session's terminal (iTerm, Terminal, tmux, WezTerm or kitty), or jump to the tab.

ASKS

A permission

See the exact command or edit, then Allow or Deny, or deny and tell it what to do instead.

ASKS

A question

Pick one of its options with a click or a number key, or type your own answer.

Steps in the chat

See what it did, not just that it's working

Between your message and the reply, each step is one line: what it read, edited or ran, and how that went. Open a step to see the command's output or the edit's diff.

  • Failed commands stand out in red; +12 −1 shows the size of an edit
  • Live while it works, folded away once a turn is done
  • Read from the session's own transcript when you look; nothing is copied or kept

Set up

Connect your agents in one click

On first launch Cue lists the agents it finds on your Mac. Connect adds its hooks to each agent's settings, after backing the file up; your other settings and hooks stay exactly as they were.

Privacy

Everything stays on your Mac

Cue collects nothing and sends nothing anywhere. It's open source, so you can check.

No accounts, no trackingNo sign-up, no analytics, no crash reporting.
Local onlyIt talks to your agents through a socket that only processes on your Mac can reach.
Your data is a folderHistory, drafts and settings live in one folder in your Library. Delete it and it's gone.
One request outThe only time Cue goes online is to check GitHub for a newer version of itself.
